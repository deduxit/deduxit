`cargo deny check advisories` (online) found nothing.

**Why itoa came later.** On the old base (`mwlpnpvl`) with Rust 1.99.1,
itoa 1.0.19 moved the ratchet's `read-json` journey from 12 993 186 to
13 129 951 instructions (+1.05 %). That ceiling was then 12 799 833, so
the journey was 2.58 % over, past the 2 % tolerance.
- itoa alone moved it. In local builds the count was 12 888 930 without
  it, 12 915 603 with it, and 12 888 747 with itoa held.
- callgrind's per-function counts of the two local builds differ in one
  function only, glibc's `__memcmp_avx2_movbe` (454 782 -> 481 638).
  Every function of deduxit and serde_json counts the same.
- itoa 1.0.19 only swaps bounds-checked indexing for `get_unchecked_mut`
  in its formatting code, which the measured part (`serde_json::from_str`
  of a sequent) never runs.

So the smaller code moved where static data lands, and memcmp took
another path on the new alignment: layout, not slower code. On the
rebased head with Rust 1.100, read-json counts 12 400 183 with itoa
1.0.19, 5 % under `main`'s ceiling (13 059 079, raised by 54s-c3), so
itoa went in.

What Rust 1.100 forced:
- **One new lint**: clippy's `needless_range_loop` at
  `core/src/linear/nets/mell/desequentialize.rs:441`. The loop now runs
  over `self.premises.iter().enumerate()` in place of `0..n` with
  `self.premises[v]`. `premises` and `occurrence` are pushed together in
  `make`, so the length is `n` and the loop visits the same vertices in
  the same order. No rustdoc or wasm warnings.
- **The ratchet**: `derivation-chain-64` went 9 889 940 -> 9 933 453
  (+0.44 %). `main` was already 1.94 % over its ceiling (9 701 583), so
  the journey was 2.39 % over.
  - Local builds reproduce it (9 892 822 -> 9 936 335).
  - callgrind puts +40 785 of the +43 513 in
    `journeys::derivation::{closure#0}`, the one body into which LLVM
    inlines the whole derivation build. The rest is a few hundred
    instructions each in the checker's pass and the size estimate. No
    function stands out for a clean change to win it back.
  - The 1.100 build is faster on this journey: medians of 200 runs,
    alternated six times on core 12, 482 700 ns against 505 663 ns for
    the 1.99.1 build.
  - The planning session approved the raise on the author's behalf.
    The ceiling commit raises derivation-chain-64 to 9 933 453 and,
    with `ratchet --lower` (the nix-built harness and nixpkgs'
    valgrind, so the counts are the check's), lowers read-json to
    12 400 183, read-lltp to 28 279 238, read-tptp to 33 363 861 and
    ordinary-pigeons to 31 264 589.
- **The other journeys** against the old `main`: read-json −4.56 %,

  read-text −0.25 %. The rest rose by at most 0.54 % (check-qbf-20-2).

## What the next sessions must know

- The ratchet's tolerance was nearly spent before this run. On the old
  `main`, derivation-chain-64 was 1.94 % over its ceiling,
  search-wide-m2-256 1.76 % and read-text 1.48 %. So a codegen shift of
  half a percent failed the check, whatever its cause. Lowering the
  ceilings whose counts fall, in the commit that makes them fall, keeps
  that room.
- A dependency can move a journey's count without touching its code:
  itoa 1.0.19 moved read-json only through memcmp's alignment. Before
  blaming a crate, diff callgrind's self counts per function of two
  local builds (`deduxit-bench ratchet --only NAME --keep DIR`, then
  `callgrind_annotate`). Local counts differ from the check's by
  0.2 to 1 %, but the deltas reproduce.
- For counts that must equal the check's (`--lower`), run the nix-built
  harness with nixpkgs' valgrind: `nix build .#deduxit-bench --inputs-from
  . nixpkgs#valgrind.out`, valgrind's `bin` on `PATH`.
- Never edit the tree while `nix flake check` evaluates: a source read
  twice that changed in between fails that output's evaluation.
