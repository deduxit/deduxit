Decide, check, prove step by step and export sequents of linear logic, and ordinary logic through its translations

Usage: deduxit <COMMAND>

Commands:
  prove        Decide whether a sequent is provable, and print a proof if it is
  check        Check a proof read from JSON, as `prove --format json` writes it, or the refutation of an unprovable outcome
  grade        Grade an answer to a problem: whether it proves or refutes the sequent given, checked as `check` and `interact` check
  eliminate    Eliminate the cuts of a proof read from JSON, step by step
  interact     Prove a sequent step by step, reading commands from standard input
  interpolate  Find a Craig interpolant of a valid sequent of ordinary logic, and check it
  resolve      Refute a set of clauses by resolution or DPLL, and check the refutation
  herbrand     Prove a first-order sequent by Herbrand's theorem: ground instances of its clauses that have no model
  seq          Print a sequent, convert it to JSON, name its fragment, translate it from classical logic, or rewrite it into a normal form
  term         Check a lambda-term's type, or find and count the terms of a type
  workspace    Keep sequents and what is made from them in a workspace file
  help         Print this message or the help of the given subcommand(s)

Options:
  -h, --help     Print help
  -V, --version  Print version

Sequent syntax: `A, B |- C, D` (or `⊢`), either side may be empty.
  tensor  A * B, A ⊗ B        par   A | B, A par B, A ⅋ B
  with    A & B               plus  A + B, A ⊕ B
  linear implication  A -o B, A ⊸ B
  negation  ~A, A^            exponentials  !A, ?A
  units  1, bot, ⊥, top, ⊤, 0
  quantifiers  forall x. A, ∀x. A, exists x. A, ∃x. A, forall x y. A
  predicates  p(x, f(a)): a name bound by a quantifier is a variable, any
    other name a constant, or a function symbol before a bracket
  equality  s = t, and s != t or s ≠ t for its negation
Binding, tightest first: A^, then ~ ! ?, then *, |, &, +, then -o, which
groups to the right, and last a quantifier, which reaches right as far as
it can.

With --logic the input is ordinary logic: `a, a -> b |- b`, or a formula alone.
  and  a /\ b, a ∧ b         or   a \/ b, a ∨ b
  implies  a -> b, a → b      iff  a <-> b, a ↔ b
  not  ~a, ¬a                 constants  true, ⊤, false, ⊥
Binding, tightest first: ~, /\, \/, ->, and last <->; -> and <-> group to
the right.
