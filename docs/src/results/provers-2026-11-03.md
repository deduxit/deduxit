# The other provers on the growing sets, 2026-11-03

The growing sets of [Families that grow, 2026-11-02](growing-2026-11-02.md),
each family chosen to show where an engine or another prover is strong
or weak, run by linlog 0.2.0 and every other prover whose fragment and
mode cover them, each line to the size where it meets the limit. That
chapter and [the one of 2026-10-31](growing-2026-10-31.md) say what each
family is and what it claims; this one sets the other provers beside
linlog.

The rows are `bench/comparison/2026-11-03-growing/runs.csv`, written by
`bench/comparison.sh --growing --cores 4,5,6,7,8,9 --default-cores 12-15
--timeout 60` on 2026-11-03 from 09:29 to 09:46, with `ranking.md` and
each stream's `env.json` beside them. Six sequential streams, each
pinned to one efficiency core (CPUs 4 to 9) of an Intel Core Ultra X9
388H (16 cores, 64 GB, Linux 7.3.1), ran every tool but linlog's
default, which ran as a user runs it on the four low-power cores (12 to
15); every run had 60 s and 4 GiB and was measured by BenchExec's
`runexec` 3.35, one run per problem, the time being the whole process's
(its start included). linlog was built from commit `540755d87f9a`,
whose engines are those of the `v0.2.0` tag; every other tool is at the
version the flake pins. It ran by day: another project had
light work on CPUs 10 and 11, and nothing kept other processes off the
streams' cores.

linlog's two columns are the command: `linlog prove --deterministic`
on one thread, `linlog-default` as a user runs it. Like every other
tool, it gets the sequent alone, not the copy bound a family states, so
it deepens its own; a size not decided ends its line, and the line's
larger sizes are not run. 3 068 rows: 2 193 sizes not run past a line's
end, 226 outside a tool's fragment or mode, and 649 runs.

- **`contraction`**: the provable #0 linlog to 128 atoms (47 s on one

## The wrong refutations

Click & coLLecT and APLL's LL search refute `wide-m1` at every size of
its growing set, 256 to 32 768 pairs, in 6 ms to 37 s, while linlog
proves it to 4 096 with a checked proof; in the run of 2026-10-15 they
refuted it and its shapes with shared atoms from 256 on, and timed out
from 8 to 36. To find out whether our translation is the cause, the
files the harness writes for each tool were run by hand on the tools'
binaries (the flake's `.#comparison`), at every size from 1 pair to
512, and on sequents written by hand. Derived from those runs and from
the tools' sources at the revisions the flake pins:

- **The answer flips at 63 pairs, a sequent of 64 formulas.** Both tools
  prove `wide-m1` at 1 to 4 pairs, reach their limits (10 s and 12 s by
  hand) at every size tried from 8 to 62 pairs, and refute it at 63 and
  64 within 10 ms, and at every larger size tried; the time then grows
  with the sequent (37 s for Click & coLLecT at 32 768 in the run).
- **It is the number of formulas beside a focused `⊗`, not the atoms or
  the depth.** One atom does it: `⊢ p ⊗ p ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ p, p⊥, …, p⊥`, with 63
  factors and 63 negations (64 formulas), is refuted by both in under
  10 ms, and with 62 both reach the limit. 63 negated atoms beside a
  balanced tensor of depth 6 are refuted too, while `⊢ ⊥, …, ⊥, 1` of 64
  formulas is proved, its `⊥` leaving the context before any `⊗`.
  `partition-yes`, refuted in 2026-10-15's run at 24 and 28, has 75 and
  87 formulas once its `⅋` are taken apart, and 63 at 20, where both
  tools reached the limit.
- **The cause is an integer overflow in the search, shared by both
  tools.** Each enumerates the ways to split the context `Γ` of a
  focused `A ⊗ B` by counting down from `2^|Γ| − 1`, computed as
  `fast_exp_2 (List.length gamma) - 1` (Click & coLLecT's
  `focused_proof.ml`, line 280; APLL's `foc_ll_bwd.ml`, line 157, and
  its ILL search's `foc_ill_bwd.ml`, line 72, with the same count for a
  `⊸` focused on the left at line 163). OCaml's native
  integers have 63 bits, so `2^63` is 0 and the count starts at −1 for
  any context of 63 formulas or more: the `⊗` rule then has no premise,
  the search fails everywhere, and the tool reports a search it calls
  exhaustive as a refutation. With 62 formulas the count is the largest
  integer and the search runs on.
- **The translation is cleared**: the hand-written one-atom sequent
  shows the same flip without the harness, and the files the harness
  wrote read as the sequent linlog proves.

The same counter accounts for APLL's ILL search on the library's Petri
nets in the run of 2026-10-15 (derived from that run's rows and the
library's files, not run again): it refuted every net whose initial
marking has 63 tokens or more and on which it did not crash (1 327,
1 296 of them proved by linlog with checked proofs; 13 larger nets, of
401 to 20 000 tokens, ended in a stack overflow) and none with fewer. So every wrong verdict of the
two tools in both runs is this overflow.

both ran, the tool's time across and linlog's up, both logarithmic; a
problem not solved, a wrong verdict included, lies on the dashed line
of the 60 s limit. A point below the diagonal is one linlog solved
