# The baseline of 2026-11-02

The fourth baseline of deduxit's engines, the engines of 0.2.0, taken by
`bench/baseline.sh` on 2026-11-02 from 14:34 for 2 h 23 min, at commit
`a917eaf4772d`, in a release build on an Intel Core Ultra X9 388H (16
cores, 62 GB). A time is the median of up to three runs. Every proof of
the baseline passed the checker, and no verdict differs from the third
baseline's on any problem both decide. The tables of every run are
[bench/RESULTS.md](https://github.com/deduxit/deduxit/blob/main/bench/RESULTS.md),
the rows `bench/results/2026-11-02/`, and the four baselines compared
[bench/COMPARISON.md](https://github.com/deduxit/deduxit/blob/main/bench/COMPARISON.md),
from which the tables below are taken.

It was taken by day, beside other work, and not on the third's cores:

- the sequential runs went in four streams at once, each pinned to a CPU
  of its own, CPUs 4, 5, 2 and 3 where the third's were 1, 0, 2 and 3:
  the passes under one bias (`lltp-rarer`, `lltp-forward`), the long
  runs and two of the last stage's reruns
  (`lltp-intuitionistic-generous`, `lltp-classical-generous`) went on
  two efficiency cores, the bias passes taking about 1.3 to 1.6 times
  the third's time for that; the families, the engines and the other
  LLTP passes ran on the third's cores;
- other processes could share the streams' CPUs (46 problems waited
  for a CPU for over 1 % of their time, `†` in the tables);
- the parallel stage ran on 16 threads over the whole machine while two
  other projects' sessions worked on it, so it is reported apart below
  and is not comparable with the third baseline's.

## The LLTP library

Problems solved within 5 s on one thread, per pass (the counts of
`bench/RESULTS.md`'s "Solved within the time limit", summed per file):

| pass | solved | the third |
|---|--:|--:|
| `lltp-intuitionistic` (4 495 problems, copy bound 3; CPU 2) | 3 663 | 3 610 |
| `lltp-classical` (4 512; CPU 3) | 3 674 | 3 621 |
| `lltp-rarer` (the backward search alone; CPU 4, an efficiency core) | 3 662 | 3 608 |
| `lltp-forward` (the forward search alone, 30 copies; CPU 5, an efficiency core) | 4 135 | 4 026 |
| `lltp-copies-10` (1 003 that ended at the copy bound; CPU 2) | 713 | 592 |
| `lltp-recursion` (995 that ended at the recursion limit; CPU 3) | 895 | 895 |

The Petri nets are unchanged, 3 071 of 3 137 in each pass; the gains
are in the SYJ and KLE collections, by two things new in 0.2.0, as the
rows' engines and verdicts say: the refutation by the sequent's
classical reading after a search that ended at its copy bound (53 more
in the default and the classical copy-bound-3 passes, 54 under `--bias
rarer`), and the inverse method's turns beside the
two-sided engine on a goal with an exponential (100 of `lltp-forward`'s
problems that ended at the time limit before, and with the classical
reading 121 of `lltp-copies-10`'s). Each such refutation is an
assignment falsifying the classical reading, so it holds whatever the
file's header says; the one new verdict against a header is `KLE083+1`
of `KLE-01`, whose other translations were refuted before.

## The parallel stage, not comparable

The runs on more than one thread, `parallel.csv` (the families on 1, 2,
4, 8 and 16 threads, 120 s), `parallel-net.csv` (the net engine's),
`lltp-all-cores.csv` (1 102 LLTP problems on every core, 5 s) and
`lltp-default.csv` (the library under the command's default, 2 s), ran
on 16 threads over the whole machine (the rows' `jobs`) while other
work used it, where the third baseline's ran at night: their times,
speedups and the problems decided within the limit measure that load as
much as the engines. A rerun of this stage on a quiet machine replaces
these four files and this section.

| pass | solved | the third |
|---|--:|--:|
| `lltp-all-cores` (1 102, every core, 5 s) | 993 | 993 |
| `lltp-default` (4 495, the command's default: no copy bound, one thread for 0.1 s and then a pool of the other fifteen, 2 s) | 4 124 | 4 024 |

Under the default, 104 problems that ended at the limit before are
decided by the inverse method's turns, and four that the third decided
within 2 s end at the limit (`SYJ205+1.008` and `SYJ212+1.012` to
`.014` of the cbn translation), one of them with about 6.5 of its 16
threads' worth of CPU time. One thing in `parallel.csv` holds whatever
the load: on `mix`, one thread decides 8 to 11 pairs in about 0.27 to
0.30 s by the inverse method's turn, which the dispatch gives only to a
search of one thread, while 2 to 16 threads race the focused engine
alone and take 0.8 s at 8 pairs, about a minute and more at 10, and
past the 120 s limit at 11.
