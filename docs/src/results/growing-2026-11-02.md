# Families that grow, 2026-11-02

The growing sets of [Families that grow, 2026-10-31](growing-2026-10-31.md)
again, at the sizes extended since and with the classical shape of
`duplicate`, from the engines of 0.2.0: each family chosen with a claim,
the strength or the weakness of one engine or of another prover, which
the growth of the time to decide the family shows. That chapter says
what each family is and what its claim and comparison are;
`linlog-bench families` prints each family's sizes and claim.

## The engines

The times are `bench/growing/2026-11-02/`: on 2026-11-02 from 16:59 to
17:28, `bench/growing.sh --cores 2,3 --pool-cores 2-3 --timeout 60`,
two sequential streams pinned to CPUs 2 and 3, two performance cores of
an Intel Core Ultra X9 388H, and the pool pass on both of them (so "2
threads"), with the build of linlog that took
[the baseline of the same day](baseline-2026-11-02.md) (commit
`a917eaf4772d`), one run per problem. It ran by day, right after that
baseline, while two other projects' sessions worked on the machine's
efficiency cores; nothing kept other processes off CPUs 2 and 3.

**Time against size.** The search time of each family's shape against
its size, both logarithmic, a line per configuration: the default and
every engine that admits the shape, forced; the memo switched off, no
copy bound and two threads dashed in their engine's colour; a size not
decided within 60 s a hollow marker on the limit's line; a size not run
because the line met the limit at a smaller one a small cross above it.

What it shows is what the run of 2026-10-31 showed: every line that
both runs drew ends at the same size, its largest decided size taking
within a quarter of the earlier time where that was over a millisecond,
apart from the sizes added since; so each claim stands as that chapter
states it. What is new, each from the rows:

- `mix`: the inverse method forced decides 4 096 pairs in 87 ms, and
  the default, after its turn's 2²⁴ units of work, 4 096 in 0.39 s,
  flat from 8 pairs on; the focused engine alone grows about eightfold
  per pair, as before.
- `chain`: the inverse method forced decides 8 192 clauses in 19 ms,
  linear; the default and the focused engine still meet the recursion
  limit at 1 024.
- `contraction` #0: the inverse method forced decides 256 atoms in
  1.8 s and meets its memory bound at 512.
- `lambek-fo` #1, refuted, takes 1.1 s at 2 048 formulas.
- `duplicate` #1, the same sequent classically: the Horn engine decides
  262 144 factors in 63 ms, as the default does; the focused engine
  forced 256 in 0.22 s and passes the minute at 1 024; the inverse
  method forced takes 44 s at 16 and passes the minute at 32.

## The other provers

The other provers ran on these growing sets to their limits on
2026-11-03, beside the engines of the same release:
[the other provers on the growing sets, 2026-11-03](provers-2026-11-03.md)
has their figure of time against size, their fitted growth and their
cactus and scatter plots. It adds where each family's claim about
another prover shows (no other tool passes 8 factors of `duplicate` but
Sympli), what they decide that linlog does not (LinearOne 8 problems of
`lambek-fo` and `witness-fo`), and why Click & coLLecT and APLL refute
`wide-m1`: an overflow in their search from 63 formulas beside a `⊗`.
The check of every tool on each set's first two sizes is
[the chapter of 2026-10-31](growing-2026-10-31.md#the-other-provers),
and the comparison with the other provers on the libraries is
[that of 2026-10-15](provers-2026-10-15.md), of the engines of 0.1.0.
